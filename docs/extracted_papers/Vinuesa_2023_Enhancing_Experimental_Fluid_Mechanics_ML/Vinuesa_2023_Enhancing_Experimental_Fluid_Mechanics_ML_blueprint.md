This manuscript is a perspective review paper titled *"The transformative potential of machine learning for experiments in fluid mechanics"* by Ricardo Vinuesa, Steven L. Brunton, and Beverley J. McKeon. Instead of proposing or training a single specific ML flow model or simulating a specific flow regime, the paper reviews and categorizes the broader landscape of machine learning applied to experimental fluid mechanics into three primary domains:
1. **Augmenting Measurement Fidelity:** Correcting sensor bias/imperfections (e.g., Pitot tubes, hot wires), data imputation, denoising, and super-resolution reconstruction of optical and field measurements (e.g., PIV, smoke-wire visualization).
2. **Experimental Design, Reduced-Order Modeling, and Digital Twins:** Shape optimization of experimental setups (e.g., via Gaussian processes), data-driven reduced-order models (DMD, SINDy), uncertainty quantification (UQ), and multi-fidelity digital twins.
3. **Flow Estimation and Real-Time Control:** Estimating full flow fields from sparse wall sensors (e.g., via CNNs, GANs) and active/passive flow control (e.g., drag reduction via deep reinforcement learning).
